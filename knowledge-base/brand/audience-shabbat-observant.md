# Audience: The Religious, Shabbat-Observant Market

**This is the most important file in the knowledge base.** It constrains scheduling, imagery, language, timing, and tone across every channel. Read it before writing any copy or planning any campaign.

## Who we are talking to

Primarily **religious, Shabbat-observant Jewish households in Israel** (Dati Leumi and Haredi segments, plus traditional/*masorti* families who keep Shabbat). Practically, the ideal customer is:

- A **household manager furnishing a family home** — often a woman making or heavily influencing furniture decisions — in a family that hosts frequently.
- **Values-driven, not price-driven** on things that carry *kavod* (honor): the Shabbat/Chag table is one of them.
- **Large families and frequent guests** — furniture must endure heavy, weekly use.
- Thinks in terms of **the long term and the family** — an heirloom mindset fits naturally.
- **Highly active on WhatsApp**; community word-of-mouth and recommendation matter enormously.

### Segments to tailor to
| Segment | Nuance |
|---|---|
| **Dati Leumi** | Design-aware, modern aesthetic, comfortable with Instagram/TikTok, English/Hebrew mix ok. Aspirational, tasteful. |
| **Haredi** | More conservative imagery and language; less TikTok, more WhatsApp/word-of-mouth/print-adjacent; modesty (*tzniut*) in visuals is essential; avoid anything that reads as secular-trendy. |
| **Masorti / traditional** | Keeps Shabbat and Chagim, values a beautiful table, broad appeal. |

When in doubt, **default to the more conservative choice** — it rarely offends the modern segment, whereas the reverse can alienate the traditional one.

## Cultural themes that resonate (the emotional core of our marketing)

1. **The Shabbat / Chag table as the heart of the home.** The table where the family gathers weekly is sacred and central. Beautiful, dignified seating around it is *hiddur mitzvah* — beautifying a mitzvah — not indulgence.
2. **Kavod Shabbat (honoring the Sabbath).** Investing in the Shabbat experience is a value, not a luxury. "The King's chairs for the Sabbath Queen."
3. **Hachnasat orchim (welcoming guests).** Hosting is a core mitzvah and social identity. Seating that comfortably and beautifully receives guests speaks directly to this.
4. **L'dorot — for generations.** Durability and the heirloom quality map onto a culture that thinks in generational continuity.
5. **Simcha & family gatherings** — Shabbat, Yom Tov, and lifecycle celebrations all center on a well-set, well-seated table.

These are **sincere** appeals. They must never feel like a marketer cynically borrowing religious language. Correctness and reverence are the price of admission.

## Non-negotiable scheduling rule: Shabbat & Chag

**Never run ads, publish posts, or send automated messages during Shabbat or a Chag (Yom Tov).**

- **Shabbat:** from candle-lighting (~18–40 min before sunset Friday) until *tzeit hakochavim* / Motzei Shabbat (nightfall Saturday, ~40–72 min after sunset).
- **Chagim (Yom Tov):** Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, first & last days of Sukkot, Shmini Atzeret/Simchat Torah, first & last days of Pesach, Shavuot — same rule as Shabbat.
- Practical implementation: **pause paid campaigns before Shabbat/Chag and reactivate after.** See `strategy/annual-calendar-jewish.md` for the exact operational schedule and the reasons (respect **and** performance — the audience is offline, so spend is wasted).

Getting this right is also a **trust signal**: an advertiser who visibly respects Shabbat is instantly more credible to this audience.

## Language & imagery rules

**Language**
- Hebrew-first, always. Warm, respectful register.
- Use religious/Shabbat terminology **correctly**: *כבוד שבת*, *שבת המלכה*, *הכנסת אורחים*, *סעודת שבת/חג*, *הידור*, *לדורות*. If unsure of correct usage, ask before publishing.
- Avoid slang that reads as secular-trendy for the Haredi segment.

**Imagery**
- **Tzniut (modesty)** in any human depiction — especially for Haredi-facing creative. Prefer product-focused, table-focused, and process shots. If people appear, dress and framing must be modest.
- Shabbat/Chag styling (challah, candles, a set table) is powerful **when tasteful and correct** — e.g., don't show lit candles paired with a "buy now / online checkout on Shabbat" message.
- Show the product in a **warm family home**, not a cold showroom.

## Objections & motivations (for messaging)

**Why they buy:** honor of the table/home, durability for heavy family use, custom fit, "do it once, do it right," recommendation from someone they trust, an upcoming Chag or simcha.

**Why they hesitate:** price (premium), lead time (will it be ready before the Chag?), trust (is the quality real / will they deliver?), decision complexity (fabrics, sizes).

Every one of these hesitations has an answer in `creative/messaging-framework.md` — lead time, warranty, before/after proof, studio visit, and personal WhatsApp service all defuse them.
